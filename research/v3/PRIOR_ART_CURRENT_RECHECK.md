# V3 focused prior-art recheck

## Result

The contribution must not be framed as the first accessibility-conversion benchmark or as a discovery that prior work is absent. Current DocAccessible materials describe a public, evidence-traceable document-conversion research program, including an accessible HTML conversion benchmark and a 50-document public conversion corpus. DAISY reports a 2026 benchmark of seven PDF-to-accessible-document services using shared test documents and a quality framework. These are direct threats to broad novelty language.

Relevant adjacent work also includes destination-only PDF accessibility evaluation (Kumar et al., ASSETS 2025, <https://doi.org/10.1145/3663547.3746380>), PDF-to-HTML accessibility reconstruction (SciA11y, <https://doi.org/10.1145/3441852.3476545>), automated PDF tagging/remediation (iTagPDF, <https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790289>), and office-document accessibility conversion work by Roig and Ribera. Microsoft’s current exporter documentation further shows that real office exporters map source concepts to PDF structure such as `Figure`, `Artifact`, `Caption`, `Link`, `Note`, and `Formula`.

## Narrow distinction that survives

The defensible contribution is a controlled source-grounded DOCX-to-PDF preservation protocol: verified native DOCX contracts, identical frozen sources across named conversion pipelines, feature-specific destination evidence requirements, explicit abstention for unresolved cross-format equivalence, and provenance-linked test artifacts. This is a protocol distinction, not a claim of priority or exclusivity.

## What is not claimed

The paper does not claim to outperform DocAccessible or DAISY, to benchmark PDF-to-HTML conversion, to build a destination accessibility evaluator, to remediate PDFs, or to establish user impact. The 13-fixture V3 variant set is a robustness extension, not evidence of representativeness.
