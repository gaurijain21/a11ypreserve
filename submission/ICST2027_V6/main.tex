\title{A11yPreserve: Source-Grounded Testing of Accessibility Preservation in DOCX-to-PDF Conversion}
\author{Anonymous for review}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Author-supplied accessibility information in a DOCX document can be changed, removed, or represented differently during conversion even when the destination PDF is tagged. We present A11yPreserve, a source-grounded testing protocol that freezes feature contracts, verifies source OOXML, converts identical documents through named real pipelines, extracts multiple forms of PDF evidence, and compares cross-format semantics with explicit abstention. The prospectively frozen V4 primary benchmark contains 13 accessibility feature families, five controlled variants per family, 65 atomic DOCX fixtures, and 130 fixture--pipeline cases across LibreOffice and Google Docs. A V6 rerun genuinely recomputed all 65 LibreOffice baseline controls: 0/65 classifications disagreed with the frozen analysis, with 0/65 extraction failures and 0/65 normalized evidence differences. A richer destination mutation audit found 59 valid applied mutants, 46/59 coarse-classification detections, and 10 semantically meaningful survivors; an additive evidence-drift alert flagged 59/59 valid mutants. Executable baselines detected 0/59 injected faults for tag presence and visible-text equality, 16/59 for destination-only evidence, and 46/59 for the full source-grounded method. Microsoft Word remains a post-freeze supplementary 65-observation arm. The findings are descriptive of the recorded conditions: the contribution is an auditable testing methodology for selected machine-verifiable semantics, not a complete accessibility assessment or universal converter ranking.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and Software Availability}
The anonymous submission bundle contains the manuscript sources, frozen source contracts, conversion manifests, extracted evidence, V5 historical audit records, V6 per-case traces, and reproduction scripts needed to inspect the reported calculations. V4/V5 material remains historical provenance; V6 is additive. Re-running LibreOffice requires the recorded local converter configuration; re-running Google Docs requires an authorized interactive session and is not fully offline-reproducible. No credentials, cookies, browser profiles, private account material, or natural-document corpus are distributed. The software is provided for research inspection and reproduction; final public licensing and citation metadata remain author decisions.
